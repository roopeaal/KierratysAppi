# Visual directions

## Product design subject

The subject is a Finnish person holding a package at the moment of disposal. The single primary job is to turn an uncertain physical package into a calm, component-by-component next action.

## Direction A — Material ledger (selected)

- Palette: cool mineral neutrals, deep ink, cobalt action, pine verification, amber uncertainty, brick error.
- Type: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for readable interface/body; IBM Plex Mono for GTIN, source, dates, and rule metadata.
- Layout: a quiet camera/action surface followed by component rows that read like detachable labeled strips rather than generic cards.
- Signature: a **sorting seam**—a short vertical material-colored line that visually joins a component name, its destination, and its source/status. It evokes separable package layers without a recycling-arrow cliché.

## Direction B — Kitchen label

Off-white label stock, black utility type, one red action color, large icon stamps, and tear-off-section transitions. Clear and physical, but it risks feeling like packaging art and creates too much visual noise around uncertainty/status.

## Direction C — Nordic wayfinding

Bright white/dark navy, destination pictograms, bold route arrows, and large one-handed bottom controls. Highly legible, but too close to generic transit/municipal wayfinding and less specific to multi-component packages.

## Self-critique and revision

The first Material Ledger sketch used a familiar “eco green” primary action and rounded cards. That could belong to nearly any sustainability app. The revision makes cobalt the action color, uses pine only for verified status, removes card stacking, and spends the distinctive move on the sorting seam. Sustainability is communicated by accurate material handling, not decoration.

## Selection

Material Ledger best separates physical component, action, and evidence while keeping the camera flow calm. It is distinct without adding decorative media, supports dark/large-text modes, and gives uncertainty a visible textual place.
